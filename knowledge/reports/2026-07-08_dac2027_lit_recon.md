### In-domain (EDA) precedents

1. **Rip-up and reroute is the native EDA destroy-and-repair**. The OR literature itself notes the ruin/recreate principle "was first applied in the method proposed for wiring of electronic circuits" (rip-up and reroute); LNS, Ruin-Recreate, and POPMUSIC are recognized as the same paradigm under different names (see VRP survey, arXiv:2303.04147). Classic citation: Dees & Karger, "Automated rip-up and reroute" (DAC 1982) — **DOI not verified in this pass; verify before citing**. Modern instance: **"Strategic Rip-Up and Reroute", GLSVLSI 2025, DOI 10.1145/3716368.3735162** (scite-verified).
2. **Window-based local re-optimization in MBFF banking**: Chang et al.'s sliding/expanding-window mergeable-FF search and ILP-based register merging (e.g., "Loosely coupled multi-bit flip-flop allocation for power reduction", Integration 2017, DOI 10.1016/j.vlsi.2017.02.006) are destroy-repair-*shaped* but (a) heuristically priced, (b) never framed as LNS, (c) not evaluator-exact.
3. **De-banking/re-banking as perturbation**: Synopsys patent US 11,328,109 ("Refining multi-bit flip flops mapping without explicit de-banking and re-banking") explicitly motivates *avoiding* destroy-repair because "banking, de-banking, and re-banking is computationally expensive and causes significant and unpredictable perturbation" — useful adversarial citation: our exact-priced repair directly answers that objection.
4. **Contest lineage**: ICCAD-2014/2015 incremental timing-driven placement contests; ICCAD 2025 Problem C (multi-operator incremental search post-DP).
5. **Negative search result (the important one)**: sweeps for "large neighborhood search"/"ruin and recreate"/"destroy and repair" × {placement, legalization, floorplanning, macro placement, FPGA placement, clustering, EDA} across web + arXiv + scite found **no published work applying an LNS/ruin-recreate framework to MBFF banking/clustering or FF placement, and none anywhere in placement with exact (evaluator-exact) repair pricing**. Closest ML-side items (optional citations, not verified here): DR-ALNS (ICAPS 2024), neural LNS for ILP (Song et al., NeurIPS 2020).

## Threat / Opportunity assessment

**Threats.** (1) The ICCAD 2025 Problem B rerun means a second wave of contest-winner papers is in the pipeline for DAC/ICCAD 2026–27; a DAC 2027 submission that only benchmarks on the 2024 suite will look stale — we should report both 2024 and 2025 suites. (2) The DATE'26 agile in-placement paper already occupies the "when to bank relative to placement" territory and benchmarks against the ICCAD-2024 winner; our contribution must be positioned as *post-placement exact-priced destroy-and-repair re-optimization*, not another banking-ordering/flow paper. (3) B-Flex (DAC 2026) is an unknown — re-check at proceedings time; if it is FF *cell design*, it is orthogonal. **Nothing found contradicts our core novelty claim**: no LNS/ruin-recreate formulation for MBFF banking exists in the literature, and no placement-domain destroy-repair work prices repairs exactly against the scoring oracle. **Opportunities.** (1) The rip-up-and-reroute lineage strengthens rather than threatens the narrative — destroy-and-repair is native to EDA routing, and we can claim the first transfer to banking/placement with exact pricing, with the Shaw/Schrimpf/Ropke-Pisinger canon as methodological grounding. (2) The Synopsys patent's explicit complaint that de-bank/re-bank is "expensive and unpredictable" is a ready-made motivation paragraph: exact delta-pricing (our oracle-gap fix, 2026-07-07) is precisely what makes destroy-repair predictable. (3) The 2025 Problem B benchmark suite is a low-cost external-validity win — recover it via Wayback/organizers before the experimental campaign starts.
