\section{Introduction}

Multi-bit flip-flop (MBFF) banking merges single-bit registers into multi-bit trays that share internal clock inverters. It cuts clock dynamic power and cell area but costs placement freedom and timing slack. Post-placement banking spans power-driven merging under timing constraints~\cite{chang2010postplacement}, interval-graph formulations~\cite{shyu2013effective}, and flop trays in commercial flows~\cite{kahng2016floptray}. The ICCAD 2024 CAD Contest Problem~B~\cite{yang2024contest} turned this into a reproducible benchmark that optimizes banking and placement jointly under the four-term cost $\alpha\cdot\tns + \beta\cdot\mathrm{Power} + \gamma\cdot\mathrm{Area} + \lambda\cdot\mathrm{Density}$ ($\tns$: total negative slack), scored by an official evaluator on seven industrial-scale cases under hard legality constraints.

Nearly every published flow prices banking's committed decisions with a proxy timing model. The error does not average out: each committed merge moves pins and distorts the slack landscape that prices every later decision. We measured this compounding directly. Our baseline, a top-ranked open-source contest flow, carries a one-hop timing proxy that underestimates evaluator TNS by roughly 34\% on the hardest case. Under that proxy, cascades of mispriced merges of two 2-bit cells into one 4-bit cell ($2{\times}2\mathrm{b}{\to}4\mathrm{b}$) regress the score by up to $+254\%$, and a debank-and-rebank pass by $+134\%$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:background}). Structural rebanking after legalization then looks too dangerous a move class. We claim the opposite: with exact per-move pricing and staged, monotone acceptance, the same move class becomes strictly profitable. A controlled counterfactual (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}) puts the price of proxy quotes at up to $0.9\%$ of final score.

We therefore rebuild the refinement stage around exact pricing. Upstream we keep the open-source baseline flow from debanking through detailed placement. We previously strengthened this front end under the same proxy pricing (Sec.~\ref{sec:background}); those enhancements are not claims of this paper. Everything downstream of detailed placement is the contribution here:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An incremental timing oracle.} Evaluator-exact and side-effect-free, it prices any candidate move against the official cost function without mutating placement state. Incremental and full recomputation agree exactly in every round, and side-effect-free deltas match a real apply-and-revert to floating-point precision (Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle:validation}).
\item \textbf{Exactly re-priced batch local search.} Batched local search normally commits only moves that touch disjoint timing cones, a safety filter that keeps about 9\% of improving candidates per round (Sec.~\ref{sec:bitrepair}). Exact re-pricing at apply time makes the filter unnecessary and commits an order of magnitude more: a stale quote can only cause a rejection.
\item \textbf{Monotone-accept structural rebanking with rollback staging.} The engine stages merges ($2{\times}2\mathrm{b}{\to}4\mathrm{b}$, $4{\times}1\mathrm{b}{\to}4\mathrm{b}$) outside the committed netlist, prices them exactly, commits only on strict improvement, and rolls back completely otherwise.
\item \textbf{Density-term pricing combined with exact timing pricing.} A repair pass evicts flip-flops from violating bins. It accepts an eviction chain only if the exact timing plus density delta improves total cost, so refinement sees all four cost terms.
\end{itemize}

The rebuild also removed a silent restriction: a stale bookkeeping flag had been excluding 32\% of one hidden case's flip-flops from refinement altogether (Sec.~\ref{sec:operators}).

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the framework. One configuration, unchanged across all seven benchmarks, beats the contest top-3 best entry on every case; ours is the first published method to do so. The composite ratio against the contest top-3 is 0.953, versus 0.991 for the contest winner's method under the same baseline; it self-reports 0.988 against first place in a two-page late-breaking result~\cite{li2025lbr}. Ours is the first full-length method paper at this quality level on this benchmark. TIMBER~\cite{dassarma2026timber} reports strong runtime and memory results on the same cases but is not evaluator-comparable (Sec.~\ref{sec:mainresults}). Every solution we report passes the official evaluator and both legality checkers.
