\subsection{Design Goal}
\label{sec:oracle:goal}

The oracle prices every candidate move under the evaluator's own timing model: per-sink displacement delay proportional to the two-point HPWL between driver and sink pins, under one ideal clock~\cite{yang2024contest}. It needs three properties at once. \emph{Exactness}: the reported $\Delta\tns$ equals what the evaluator would measure. \emph{Side-effect freedom}: pricing mutates no netlist or timing state, so thousands of candidates can be screened without apply--revert churn. \emph{Locality}: cost scales with the affected timing cone, not the design.

Incremental STA engines meet the last two goals for full industrial timing~\cite{huang2015opentimer,huang2021opentimer2}. The contest model further permits closed-form per-sink recomputation at cone cost, which we exploit. The same simplicity explains why proxies are common. The inherited flow uses a one-hop slack approximation, the DATE~2026 flow a local critical-path wirelength proxy~\cite{chen2026agile}, and~\cite{li2025lbr} leaves pricing unspecified. None closes the loop with the scoring function. On the hardest benchmark, the one-hop proxy underestimates $\tns$ by roughly 34\%; this mispricing makes structural rebanking cascade (Sec.~\ref{sec:background}). The oracle removes the model error entirely instead of tightening the approximation.

\subsection{Timing State Keyed by Logical Objects}
\label{sec:oracle:keying}

Banking creates and destroys physical cells, so any timing cache keyed by physical instances breaks on exactly the moves that need accurate pricing.

The oracle instead keys all topology caches by \emph{immutable logical objects}: the single-bit flip-flops of the debanked netlist and the combinational gates. Banking never changes either set; a merge only remaps logical bits to a new (cell, slot) pair. Fanin and fanout cones are computed once over these objects and survive every structural mutation. Mutable leaf attributes are never cached. A bit's D/Q pin coordinates and Q-pin (clock-to-Q) delay are read live through a logical$\to$physical indirection: bit $\to$ cell and slot $\to$ library pin offset plus cell position. A structural move updates one indirection entry; no cache is rebuilt.

\subsection{Three Evaluation Modes}
\label{sec:oracle:modes}

Three entry points share one cone-propagation core.

\emph{Full pass.} Recompute every sink slack from scratch in one parallel sweep. This is the validation ground truth, linear in design size.

\emph{Incremental cone recompute.} After a move commits, repropagate only the cones reachable from the touched pins and fold the updates into the committed caches, keeping committed state exact at cone cost.

\emph{Side-effect-free delta.} Price a hypothetical move without touching committed state (Fig.~\ref{fig:oracle_delta}). The oracle builds a local \emph{override map} holding each moved bit's hypothetical D/Q positions and Q-pin delay, collects the affected sinks, walks their forward cones override-first into thread-local scratch, and returns $\alpha\cdot\Delta\tns$; the scratch is discarded. Nothing writes committed state, so arbitrarily many candidates can be priced concurrently against it. The operators of Section~\ref{sec:operators} screen candidates in parallel through this mode and re-price serially at apply time.

\subsection{Exactness Validation}
\label{sec:oracle:validation}

Validation builds check two invariants continuously. After every refinement round, the incrementally maintained $\tns$ total is compared against a full-pass recomputation; the difference is $0.000000$ on every round of every benchmark. Each side-effect-free delta is also compared against the measured delta of applying and reverting that move; the maximum discrepancy is $7.4\times10^{-13}$, from floating-point summation order. The oracle is not a tighter estimate; it implements the declared scoring semantics to floating-point precision. Externally, the maintained totals track the official evaluator within $0.05\%$ (median) at stage-boundary checkpoints on all seven cases; the maximum residual is $1.1\%$ on the most TNS-weighted states. Controlled single-cell probes localize this residual to bounded-depth slack-state updates on committed moves, not to the delta semantics, which match the evaluator to $10^{-5}$. Regardless, the evaluator binary itself scores every reported result (Sec.~\ref{sec:mainresults}), so no claim here rests on the residual.

\subsection{From Bit Swaps to Structural Remaps}
\label{sec:oracle:remaps}

We first built the delta evaluator for same-cell bit swaps, where only pin positions change. The override map generalizes it at no extra cost. Remapping a \emph{group} of bits to a new cell adds one field, the target cell's Q-pin delay override, because a new cell type also changes clock-to-Q delay. One mechanism therefore prices the whole move spectrum: slot swaps, relocations, and group-to-new-cell structural merges. The oracle-priced rebanking of Section~\ref{sec:operators} can thus screen structural candidates exactly before any netlist mutation.

Exactness is defined against the benchmark's declared cost model, but nothing in the framework depends on that model's simplicity. The operators of Section~\ref{sec:operators} see only one interface: a priced $\Delta$ for a hypothetical move. Under richer delay models, an incremental signoff-grade engine~\cite{huang2021opentimer2} can fill that interface, trading some pricing fidelity for generality.
